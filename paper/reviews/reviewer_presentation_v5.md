## Priority order

### 1. (P0) Abstract: the per-sample clause overclaims, and two sentences conflict
v5's abstract keeps "temperature" on the strength of one clause: "[shrinkage's] per-sample contraction differs from Euler's, which resembles temperature's". I computed that comparison with joint clip-bootstrap intervals (60 clips, three models pooled, 2000 draws; script in item 2):

| at the spread of | median ratio: Euler / temp. at τ_eff / Shrink | share contracted: Euler − temp. |
|---|---|---|
| E₄ | 0.67 / 0.71 / 0.54 (constant) | **+7.0 pts [4.3, 9.8]** |
| E₁₀ | 0.85 / 0.85 / 0.78 (constant) | **+4.7 pts [1.8, 7.7]** |

The median difference at E₄ is −0.04 [−0.07, −0.01]. Euler is therefore *much closer* to temperature than to shrinkage, but it is distinguishable from temperature per sample, and both intervals exclude zero. "Resembles" is defensible. Any reading of it as "is" is not, and the title invites that reading. The abstract also says both "The match holds for rates over clips, not for individual samples" and "per-sample contraction … resembles temperature's". A reader cannot tell which per-sample statement wins, because one is about decisions and the other about contraction ratios.

Drop-in abstract (all macros exist; ≈180 words; no ">100%"):
```latex
Flow-matching driving policies are made faster by integrating with fewer Euler steps, and the change is usually reported as preserving or improving accuracy. We show that this comparison is confounded by a hidden change in sampling temperature. One Euler step returns the conditional mean, and more steps contract samples toward it by an amount that a closed-form Gaussian factor matches at the median. On \NClips{} clips from the PhysicalAI-AV test split, the released 10-step samplers of three Alpamayo generations share an effective temperature of \AOneFiveETenTau--\ROneETenTau. An accurate reference solver run at that temperature reproduces their gain in single-sample error and their rate of changed driving decisions, with flip residuals of at most \FlipResidMaxAbs{} points; second-order Heun samplers with the released budget change \AOneFiveHeunFiveFlipResid--\AOneFiveHeunThreeFlipResid{} points more decisions than their matched temperature and fail the test. A uniform shrinkage toward the mean also passes this rate-level test, but per sample Euler's contraction is far closer to temperature's than to the shrinkage's. We therefore propose that accuracy gains from fewer steps be reported against a reference solver at matched effective temperature, and we give a protocol and its statistics for doing so.
```

Intro ¶3. Insert after "…minADE$_6$ get worse.", before the teaser. This puts the identification caveat on p. 1 instead of p. 4:
```latex
Our claim is about evaluation. At the level of rates over clips, a few-step Euler sampler cannot be told apart from the reference solver at a lower temperature, nor from a uniform shrinkage toward the mean with the same spread (\cref{find:shrink}); per sample, its contraction is much closer to temperature's. Temperature is the explicit and tunable member of this family, so it is the right baseline, and gains it reproduces should not be credited to the sampler. Second-order samplers at the same budget change decisions without contracting and fail the comparison (\cref{find:heun}), so the test is not vacuous.
```
