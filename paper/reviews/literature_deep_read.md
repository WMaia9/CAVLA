## Overview

### Novelty map: what is already in the literature

| Our claim (main.tex) | Prior art found | Severity |
|---|---|---|
| "One Euler step returns the conditional mean" (Sec. 3) | **Stated verbatim** in Liu et al. 2023, Fig. 5 caption: "it generates the mean of π1 when simulated with a single Euler step". Their Tab. 1a: one-step 1-RF on CIFAR-10 has recall 0.0. EDM Sec. 3 says the same: "a single Euler step to t = 0 yields the denoised image". | **High** if presented as ours. It is currently uncited. |
| Gaussian contraction factor ∏cos θ_i ≤ 1 (Eq. 1) | Claustre et al. give the Euler terminal law per Gaussian direction, q = ∏(1+h a_k) (their Eq. 8), and the inequality y_k ≤ φ(t_k) in the proof of Prop. 2. Their mixing angle θ_t is exactly the angle of our u_d(t), so our θ_{d,i} is the increment of their θ_t. The same per-step map, (c²+t_{i-1}t_i)/(c²+t_i²), appears in Stancevic Thm C.1, via Sabour et al. 2024. | **Medium.** The closed form is a corollary of known results; it must be credited, not claimed. |
| Euler pulls samples toward the centre; second-order solvers do not | GMFlow Sec. 4.1 (2D checkerboard): "samples generated by first-order solvers (DDPM, Euler) tend to concentrate toward the center, whereas those from second-order solvers (DPM++, UniPC) concentrate near the outer edges." | **Medium.** This is a qualitative anticipation of Findings 1 and 3 in a toy setting. Cite it as such. |
| Few deterministic steps reduce diversity and shift class proportions | Raya & Ambrogioni Sec. 5.1: 5-step DDIM on CelebA64 biases "race" toward the majority class; sample entropy rises with DDIM steps (Fig. 6). | **Medium.** This is the image analogue of decision-rate shifts. It is not in our "Discretization and diversity" paragraph. |
| Matched-temperature comparison to separate a gain from an entropy loss | Yue et al. 2025 Sec. 4.5 raise an RL model's sampling temperature to match base-model entropy before comparing pass@k. RL raises pass@1 and lowers pass@256, the same pattern as ADE_1 up and minADE_6 down. | **Low-medium.** It is a precedent for the protocol idea in a different field. Cite it; it strengthens the framing. |
| Proposition (reflection symmetry ⇒ Runge–Kutta keeps the decision) | Not in Biroli or Raya. The core fact, that RK/B-series maps are affine-equivariant, is classical geometric-integration material (McLachlan et al. 2016; not read here). | **Low-medium.** Claim only the decision-level corollary and cite the equivariance. |
| Matched-temperature protocol; τ_eff ≈ 0.83 shared by three Alpamayo generations; Heun fails the test; FlashDrive ≈ E_4; Qwen-Drive boundary | **Not found anywhere.** No paper measures output spread versus step count, defines an effective temperature for a sampler, or tests equivalence. None of the driving reports (Alpamayo-R1, Qwen-Drive, DiffusionDrive, GoalFlow, FlashDrive) ablates temperature or noise scale against steps with intervals. | This is the defensible contribution. |

**Bottom line.** The paper's novelty is the **protocol and the driving evidence**, not the mechanism. The theory section should open with "it is known that E_1 returns the mean (Liu; EDM) and that Euler contracts Gaussian coordinates (Claustre; Sabour/Stancevic)". The new contribution is that this contraction is *rate-equivalent to a temperature* in production driving VLAs, and that this equivalence confounds reported acceleration gains. One new theoretical consequence came out of this read and is not in any paper: **∏cos θ_i ≤ cos(π/2k)^k on any grid**. That is 0.883 for k=10, 0.729 for k=4 and 0.5 for k=2, with equality on the KL-optimal equal-angle grid. It caps how little any k-step Euler grid can contract, and it ties step schedules to temperature.

### Inaccurate or imprecise citations in main.tex (summary; details in each section)

1. **Liu et al. / Lipman et al.** (Intro and Sec. 3): the one-step-mean result is Liu's, stated in their Fig. 5. It is cited only for the interpolant, and the Sec. 3 paragraph has no citation. *Imprecise; this is an attribution problem.*
2. **Claustre et al.** (Sec. 3): "implicit in the proof of Proposition 2" is accurate but undersells the overlap. Their Eq. 8 gives the Euler factor explicitly, and their angle θ_t is our angle. *Accurate, incomplete.*
3. **GMFlow** (Related Work): "few-step error has been attributed to predicting only the mean of the velocity distribution". GMFlow says few-step underperformance is "due to discretization error" and that Gaussian transition approximations "hold only for small step sizes". The more relevant sentence, Euler concentrates toward the centre and second-order solvers toward the edges, is not used. *Imprecise.*
4. **SubFlow** (Related Work): "Few-step generators lose rare modes in image synthesis". This is true of *trained one-step generators* (MeanFlow, Shortcut). But SubFlow's own Tab. 2 shows that more Euler steps of the same MeanFlow model do **not** recover recall (43.45 at 1 NFE, 38.91 at 2, 38.02 at 128). SubFlow attributes the loss to the training objective, "rather than in the discretization error". Cited as support for a discretization effect it is *misleading*; it is closer to a counterpoint.
5. **BigGAN** (Related Work): "scaling the prior noise". BigGAN *truncates* (resamples) rather than scaling. *Imprecise.*
6. **Biroli** (Related Work): "motivate where steps should go". Biroli says nothing about steps or samplers; Raya does (late start). *Imprecise for Biroli.*
7. **AdaVLA** (Intro): "Accelerations that build on first-order integration". AdaVLA's steps are RK2-midpoint evaluations with a direct midpoint jump to t=1, not Euler. *Imprecise for AdaVLA.* ProbeFlow (whose minimum branch is exactly E_2), Coda (Euler then corrector) and FlashDrive (Euler with cache) are accurate.
8. **Alpamayo-R1** (Background): "The released policies use forward Euler with 10 uniform steps". The R1 paper documents δt = 0.1 for R1 only and itself uses 5 steps. The 2-Super card says "10 diffusion steps" and the 1.5 card is silent. *Imprecise; cite the cards and code.*
9. **Qwen-Drive** (Discussion): "positions normalized by 165 m". Only x uses 165 m; y uses 25 m and heading π/2. The residual noise at t = 0.9 is about 16.5 m longitudinally but **only about 2.5 m laterally**. *Imprecise; the lateral case needs its own check.*
10. **PhysicalAI-AV** (Data): "the PhysicalAI-AV test split" does not say which split file. The `alpamayo_aligned` split (train 276k of 306k clips) means a `default`-test clip may be in Alpamayo's training set. *Imprecise, with a validity risk.*
11. **π0** is in the bib but never cited. It uses exactly E_10 and a shifted Beta schedule that emphasizes the conditional-mean regime; it should be cited in the Background.
12. Accurate as written: FlashDrive "4th to 7th of 8 Euler steps" (it matches 0-indexed steps 3–6 in the code); ProbeFlow and AdaVLA "adapt the number of steps"; Coda "corrects a few-step endpoint"; Stancevic; Raya; DiffusionDrive; Glow; EDM (accurate but it undersells EDM's own posterior-mean and S_noise statements).

# Part A — Core papers (accelerations, Gaussian theory, few-step diversity)

### coda2026 — Fewer Steps, Better Actions: Rethinking Flow-Matching Inference for VLA Policies (arXiv:2609.21216v1, 18 Sep 2026; submitted to IEEE)
- **Access:** Full text: Secs. 1–6, Tabs. I–VII, Figs. 1–6 and the appendix.
- **What it actually shows:**
  - **Setting.** Frozen π0.5 fine-tuned on RoboTwin 2.0 (50 Easy bimanual tasks, 50 episodes per task per NFE), plus frozen official SmolVLA (10 tasks). Euler with t = 1 noise → 0 action, N ∈ {1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 10}.
  - **Method.** A lightweight Transformer "corrector", initialised from the action expert, reads the prefix KV cache, the few-step candidate a_θ and the source noise ε, and predicts a residual. It is trained with **L1** to the demonstration: Δa* = a* − sg(a_θ), with N_train = 5. The executed action is a_θ + Δa.
  - **Key numbers (Tab. I).** Base π0.5 success at NFE 1/2/3/4/5/10: **59.76/67.40/71.28/69.96/71.64/71.52%**. Coda: 69.80/71.88/73.64/73.12/74.68/72.60.
    - The 5-step gain is +3.04 pp, task-bootstrap [1.04, 5.08]; 27 tasks improve, 13 decline.
    - 13-task panel (Tab. II): base at 5/6/10 steps is 70.40/71.69/**73.02**; Coda at 5 steps is 77.38 (+5.69 over 6 steps at equal latency).
    - SmolVLA (Tab. IV): official 35.0/60.8/60.8/64.4 at 1/2/5/10; Coda 55.8/69.4/72.0/72.6. The **no-ε corrector does better**: 58.2/71.4/75.2/74.8.
    - Under visual randomisation (Hard), the gain disappears: −0.56 pp [−2.71, 1.64].
  - **Assumptions.** Single seed stream per episode; closed-loop success only. No diversity, spread or open-loop distribution metric for the base sampler.
- **Does it show that few steps are better?** **No.** The title refers to *few steps plus a learned correction*. For the frozen base policy:
  - One step is much worse (−12 pp on π0.5, −29 pp on SmolVLA).
  - Five and ten steps are tied (−0.12 pp).
  - On the independent 13-task panel, 10 steps are better than 5 (73.02 vs 70.40).
  - The paper says the plateau "motivates" reallocation; it does not claim fewer steps improve the base policy.
- **Does our temperature account explain their result?** Partly, and only the base-policy part:
  - E_1 returns the conditional mean, which in multimodal bimanual manipulation can average two valid grasps. That fits the large E_1 drop, and also our SmolVLA/LIBERO appendix note that success collapses only at very low temperature.
  - E_5 vs E_10 corresponds to roughly τ_eff 0.7 vs 0.83 by our Alpamayo numbers. A plateau in that range is what a success metric that is insensitive to τ ∈ [0.5, 1] predicts.
  - Coda's own gain cannot be read from its paper. It is a learned, demonstration-supervised residual that adds information. But its L1 objective pulls toward the conditional *median* of demonstrations given the candidate, and the better no-ε variant is a deterministic function of the candidate. Both are contraction-like. **Whether part of Coda's gain is a temperature drop is untested**: they report no spread.
- **Relation:** **Neighbours.** It does not anticipate us and does not contradict us. Its title is a direct target for our argument, but its data do not support "fewer Euler steps are better" for the base policy, so we should not attack it as if it claimed that.
- **Citing sentences in main.tex:**
  - Intro: "Accelerations that build on first-order integration~\citep{flashdrive2026,probeflow2026,adavla2026,coda2026} can inherit this temperature shift; we test one of them, FlashDrive's velocity cache." **Accurate for Coda.** It runs N Euler steps and then corrects.
  - Background: "Coda corrects a few-step endpoint~\citep{coda2026}." **Accurate.**
  - Related Work: "FlashDrive, ProbeFlow, AdaVLA and Coda reduce or correct flow sampling and report trajectory or success metrics … Our protocol separates the part of such gains that a temperature change reproduces." **Accurate.** We could be more specific: "Coda's base π0.5 policy is flat between 5 and 10 Euler steps (71.64 vs 71.52%) and loses 12 points at one step."
  - The abstract's "usually reported as preserving or improving accuracy" (uncited) is consistent with Coda's 5-vs-10 plateau only in the "preserving" sense.
- **Directions (ranked):**
  1. *(writing, 15 min)* Quote Coda's base-policy step curve as independent evidence: E_1 ≪ E_5 ≈ E_10 in closed-loop manipulation is what contraction to the mean predicts. Do not imply that Coda claims fewer Euler steps are better.
  2. *(exp., robotics, secondary; ~1–2 GPU-days with RoboTwin set up)* π0.5 at 10 steps from τz, τ ∈ {0.5, …, 1}, against E_5 and Coda. If success rises at lower τ, part of the "correction" gain is temperature. Only worth doing if the SmolVLA/LIBERO appendix is kept.
  3. *(analysis, cheap)* If Coda weights are released, measure the seed spread of a_θ + Δa against a_θ. That tells us whether correctors contract.

### probeflow2026 — ProbeFlow: Training-Free Adaptive Flow Matching for Vision-Language-Action Models (arXiv:2603.17850v3, 12 Oct 2026)
- **Access:** Full text: Secs. I–V, Algorithm 1, Tabs. I–V, Figs. 1–5.
- **What it actually shows:**
  - **Setting.** Evo-1 (InternVL3-1B and an 8-layer DiT flow head, T = 50) on MetaWorld MT50 and LIBERO. 5 seeds × 10 episodes per task. An OSC variant was also tested on π0.5, π0, SmolVLA and GR00T N1.5.
  - **Method.** Evaluate v_start = v(x_0, 0), take a probe step Δt_probe = 0.5, evaluate v_probe, and compute S = cos(v_start, v_probe). Then N = clip(N_min + ⌊(1−S)/ε⌋ ΔN, 2, 20).
    - If N = 2, the output is x_probe + 0.5·v_probe, **which is exactly E_2**.
    - Otherwise it runs an N-step Euler (N+1 NFE).
  - **Key numbers.**
    - MetaWorld (Tab. I): Euler N = 50/20/10/3/2 give **82.5/80.6/81.6/72.4/68.5%**. ProbeFlow gets 83.2% at 2.60 steps.
    - Heun is dramatically worse: **13.6% at Heun-10 (20 NFE)** and 52.0% at Heun-25. AB2-10 gives 78.8%.
    - LIBERO (Tab. II): Euler 92.5/92.3/89.0/86.9/83.2 at N = 50/20/10/5/4. ProbeFlow 88.7% at 4.53 steps; Heun-10 83.3%.
    - Most chunks get N = 2 (task-mean S ≈ 0.986–0.994).
    - OSC changes on other VLAs are mixed: −2.0 to +3.0 pp.
  - **Assumptions.** Success only; no ADE or spread. Seeds are aggregated with no paired intervals; the OSC rows have 3 seeds.
- **Accuracy gains from fewer or adaptive steps?** On LIBERO, **fixed fewer steps monotonically hurt.** On MetaWorld, Euler is flat from 10 to 50 steps and falls below that. ProbeFlow's 83.2% vs 82.5% for E_50 is within seed-to-seed noise (no interval). Its large gain over fixed E_2 (+14.7 pp) comes from routing a minority of chunks to dense schedules, since most chunks *are* E_2.
  - There is no temperature-shaped gain to explain.
  - The routing score has a natural reading in our framework. For a Gaussian target the field's direction rotates in proportion to the conditional spread: with zero conditional variance, v = m − z is constant along the path and S = 1. So high S means low conditional spread, which is exactly where contraction toward the mean costs nothing. ProbeFlow uses few steps where the temperature drop is harmless. That is a testable theoretical link.
- **Heun result.** Heun at the same or higher NFE is catastrophically worse than Euler on MetaWorld. The authors suggest the predictor–corrector's intermediate queries are off-distribution. This parallels our Finding 3 (Heun changes decisions without contracting) and is independent external evidence that "second-order ≠ better" for VLA action heads.
- **Relation:** **Neighbours, with a supporting side result** (Heun). No anticipation.
- **Citing sentences in main.tex:**
  - Intro: "Accelerations that build on first-order integration~\citep{…probeflow2026…}". **Accurate.** Both branches are Euler.
  - Background: "ProbeFlow and AdaVLA adapt the number of steps". **Accurate.**
  - Related Work: as above. **Accurate.**
- **Directions (ranked):**
  1. *(writing, 15 min)* In Finding 3 or the Discussion, cite ProbeFlow Tab. I: Heun-10 13.6% and Heun-25 52.0% against Euler-10 81.6% on MetaWorld. It shows that the Heun failure is not specific to Alpamayo.
  2. *(analysis, ~2 GPU-h on existing clips)* Compute ProbeFlow's S per clip for Alpamayo-1.5 and correlate it with the per-clip conditional spread and with per-clip E_4 contraction or flip rate. If S predicts where Euler contracts, we have a training-free per-clip τ_eff estimator. That would turn our protocol into a deployable diagnostic, and it partly answers the "Gaussian factor does not rank clips" weakness in Finding 1.
  3. *(optional exp.)* Run ProbeFlow's router on Alpamayo and place it on the temperature curve. That gives a second published acceleration tested on a driving VLA.

### adavla2026 — AdaVLA: Adaptive Step Flow Matching for Training-free Acceleration of VLA Models (IROS 2026; arXiv:2608.29208v2)
- **Access:** Full text: Secs. I–VI, Tabs. I–IV, Figs. 1–5.
- **What it actually shows:**
  - **Setting.** π0.5 and X-VLA on LIBERO (50 rollouts per task, A10 for success, Jetson AGX Orin for latency), plus SmolVLA on a real SO-ARM101 (4 tasks × 30 trials).
  - **Method, part 1: adaptive steps.** At time τ, evaluate v_τ and the midpoint v_mid at τ + (1−τ)/2, and compute the curvature C = ‖v_τ − v_mid‖/‖v_mid‖.
    - If C < C_th: jump directly to the end, A_final = A + v_mid(1−τ). This is an explicit-midpoint (RK2) step to t = 1.
    - Otherwise: advance by a bounded step using v_mid.
  - **Method, part 2: pruning.** Plus curvature-modulated MLP pruning of the VLM using a participation-ratio importance score.
  - **Key numbers (Tab. II, π0.5 LIBERO average).** Baseline 10 steps 98.65%, AdaVLA 99.45%, latency 958→513 ms.
  - **Ablation (Tab. III).**
    - "−Pruning, +Adp": 98.65%, back to baseline.
    - Static steps: 1-step 97.05%, 5-step 98.00%, random 1–10 steps 98.65%.
    - C_th 0.25: 98.40%.
  - **Real robot.** 87.5% → 90.0% (30 trials per task; not significant).
  - **Assumptions.** Success only; LIBERO is near ceiling.
- **Accuracy gains from fewer or adaptive steps?** **No.** The +0.8 pp average gain disappears when pruning is removed, so the adaptive-step component alone is accuracy-neutral. Fixed fewer steps cost 0.65 pp (5 steps) to 1.6 pp (1 step). LIBERO's ceiling makes π0.5 nearly insensitive even to E_1, consistent with our appendix note that "success [is] insensitive to τ ∈ [0.5, 1]". There is nothing temperature-shaped to explain.
- **Mechanism link.** Like ProbeFlow's S, the curvature C is small when the conditional spread is small: for a Gaussian, v changes only through the spread term. AdaVLA's "direct jump" is a midpoint step to t = 1 from wherever it fires. Applied at τ = 0 it is a one-step midpoint method. I computed its Gaussian terminal factor relative to the exact flow: 0.55 / 0.80 / 1.00 / 0.80 at s = 0.3 / 0.5 / 1 / 2. A two-step midpoint gives 0.92–0.99. So an early jump contracts, unless s ≈ 1, but a late jump hardly does. For comparison, Heun-3 gives 1.046 / 1.003 / 0.977 / 0.913 and Heun-5 gives 1.027 / 1.009 / 0.995 / 0.973, against 0.82–0.88 for E_10. **Second-order maps leave the spread near 1 and can inflate it (factor > 1 for small s).** That is the quantitative counterpart of GMFlow's "second-order solvers concentrate near the outer edges" and of our Finding 3.
- **Relation:** **Neighbours.**
- **Citing sentences in main.tex:**
  - Intro: "Accelerations that build on first-order integration~\citep{…adavla2026…}". **Imprecise.** AdaVLA uses RK2-midpoint evaluations and a midpoint jump. Suggested rewording: "Accelerations that skip or reuse velocity evaluations~\citep{…}".
  - Background: "ProbeFlow and AdaVLA adapt the number of steps". **Accurate.**
- **Directions (ranked):**
  1. *(writing, 5 min)* Fix the "first-order" wording above.
  2. *(theory, 1 h)* Extend Eq. (contraction) to the explicit midpoint and Heun maps for a Gaussian coordinate, using the numbers above: Heun-3/5 factors 0.91–1.05 vs E_10 0.82–0.88. This shows quantitatively that second-order maps barely contract, and for low-variance coordinates even expand. That is the theoretical side of Finding 3, and it pre-empts "why does Heun not contract?".

### flashdrive2026 — FlashDrive: Flash Vision-Language-Action Inference for Autonomous Driving (arXiv:2608.12932v1, 13 Aug 2026)
- **Access:** Full text: Secs. 1–5, Tabs. 1–3, App. A (Tabs. A1–A3, Figs. A1–A2). The code facts come from the previous review (`scripts/infer.py`, `flashdrive/diffusion.py`).
- **What it actually shows:**
  - **Setting.** Alpamayo 1.5-10B (and R1 in App. A.2) on 100 PhysicalAI-AV clips × 120 sliding windows (12k samples). The baseline uses an **8-step** solver ("our evaluated 8-step solver"), not the released 10.
  - **Four accelerations:**
    - streaming KV reuse with action-expert fine-tuning;
    - DFlash speculative reasoning;
    - adaptive-step flow matching: "after evaluating the first few and last few steps with fresh network calls, we replace four intermediate evaluations with the cached velocity from the preceding step";
    - W4A8 quantization.
  - **Velocity profile (Fig. 5).** The relative change between consecutive velocities is U-shaped and their cosine similarity is an inverted U (10 clips × 20 windows).
  - **Key numbers (Tab. 1).**
    - Cache alone on the system-optimized baseline: **minADE_1 1.673 → 1.536 (−0.137 m), minADE_6 0.770 → 0.812 (+0.042 m)**; action latency 113.9 → 47.6 ms.
    - Full stack: minADE_1 1.705 → 1.573 and minADE_6 0.767 → 0.844.
    - R1 (Tab. A2): 1.869 → 1.662 and 0.800 → 0.910.
    - AlpaSim, 100 clips, full stack vs baseline (Tab. 3): collision 0.19 → 0.15, off-road 0.41 → 0.32, wrong-lane 0.45 → 0.51, plan deviation 0.24 → 0.16.
  - **The authors' explanations.** For the cache: "skipping redundant mid-trajectory refinements appears to reduce accumulated numerical error". For the full stack: "streaming fine-tuning acts as a regularizer that reduces prediction variance".
  - **Assumptions.** No seeds or intervals, and no spread metric. minADE_1 is one sample per window.
- **Relation:** **Strongly supports** the paper's central example. It does not anticipate it.
  - The cache's signature (single-sample error down, best-of-6 error up) is exactly what our temperature account predicts.
  - The authors attribute it to reduced numerical error, which is the interpretation our protocol tests and rejects.
  - Their AlpaSim safety gains (fewer collisions and off-road events, smoother consecutive plans) are also what a lower temperature would plausibly produce. They come from the whole stack, not the cache alone.
- **New observation from this read: the cache is algebraically a 4-NFE Euler sampler.**
  - With fresh evaluations at steps 0, 1, 2, 7 (0-indexed) and v_2 reused on steps 3–6, the update over steps 2–7 is x_7 = x_2 + 5h·v(x_2, t_2).
  - So the cache **is** forward Euler on the non-uniform grid {0, 1/8, 2/8, 7/8, 1}: 4 NFE, with one long step of 5/8.
  - "Behaves like four Euler steps" is therefore not a coincidence of τ_eff: it *is* four Euler steps on a different grid.
  - The Gaussian factor gives a sharp, free, out-of-sample prediction for that grid. I computed it:

    | s | E_4 (uniform) | FlashDrive grid | E_8 | E_10 |
    |---|---|---|---|---|
    | 0.3 | 0.591 | 0.466 | 0.782 | 0.823 |
    | 0.5 | 0.681 | 0.410 | 0.830 | 0.862 |
    | 1 | 0.720 | 0.431 | 0.851 | 0.879 |
    | 2 | 0.681 | 0.560 | 0.830 | 0.862 |

  - The Gaussian model therefore predicts **much stronger contraction for the cache than for E_4**. Our measured τ_eff for the cache, by contrast, is close to E_4's.
  - Either the learned field is much straighter in the middle than the Gaussian model (FlashDrive's own Fig. 5 says consecutive mid-path velocities have cosine ≈ 1), or the Gaussian factor does not transfer to non-uniform grids. Either way, the paper must report this check. It is a falsification test the Gaussian story currently does not face.

### claustre2026gaussian — Gaussian Flow-Matching Schedules: Implications for Sampling and Training (arXiv:2609.25839v1, 22 Sep 2026)
- **Access:** Full text: all 11 pages, including App. A.1–A.8 (proofs).
- **What it actually shows:**
  - **Setting.** μ0 = N(0, C0) and μ1 = N(0, C1), commuting and *centered*, with independent coupling and direction-wise diagonal schedules (α_t, β_t). Polar form: α = √r cos θ, β = √(r/ρ) sin θ.
  - **Prop. 1.** The drift is b_t(x)_i = (ṙ/2r) x_i. It depends only on the variance path r_t. The mixing angle θ_t only sets the regression variance, Var(İ|I) = c r θ̇².
  - **Euler (Sec. 3).**
    - Error bound W2 ≤ (e^L/√3) h A(b)^{1/2}, with A the material-derivative energy (Eq. 7).
    - **Euler terminal variance per direction: c·q², with q = ∏_k (1 + h a_{k/N}) (Eq. 8).**
    - **Thm 1:** a necessary drift bound for exact N-step Euler.
    - **Prop. 2:** for convex φ = √r and stable steps, Euler is exact only if φ is affine (the Wasserstein geodesic). The proof (A.7) shows y_k ≤ φ(t_k), i.e. Euler *underestimates* the scale.
  - **Corollary 1.** Closed-form optimal angle schedules for training.
  - **Assumptions.** Centered commuting Gaussians, exact field, uniform grid in Thm 1 (the A.7 argument works on any grid). No experiments.
- **Does it already contain our contraction result?** **Mostly, yes, in a different notation.**
  - For our independent-coupling path, α = 1−t and β = t, so √(r_t) = ‖u_d(t)‖ with u_d = (1−t, t s_d). That is convex, and affine only when s_d = 0 (the quadratic (1−t)² + t²s² is a perfect square only then). So Prop. 2's proof gives y_k ≤ φ(t_k), i.e. contraction, on any grid.
  - Their per-step factor 1 + h a_k = 1 + h⟨u_i, u̇⟩/‖u_i‖² = ⟨u_i, u_{i+1}⟩/‖u_i‖² is exactly our step factor.
  - Their angle θ_t is the polar angle of (α, √ρ β) = u_d(t). Our θ_{d,i} is the increment θ_{t_{i+1}} − θ_{t_i}, and ∏cos θ_{d,i} is the ratio of their q to the exact factor.
  - **What they do not have:**
    - the cos-product form;
    - the reading as a contraction toward the conditional mean (their targets are centered, so the "mean" is 0 and the observation is implicit);
    - any link to temperature, decisions or learned models.
  - One more consequence follows from their angle view and is in no paper (derived here and by the theory reader). Since Σθ_i = π/2 and log cos is concave, **∏cos θ_i ≤ cos(π/2k)^k on any grid**: 0.883 for k=10, 0.729 for k=4, 0.5 for k=2. Equality holds on the equal-angle grid, which is Sabour et al.'s KL-optimal Euler schedule for Gaussians.

# Part C — Flow matching, sampler theory, temperature

*Read by the theory-cluster reader. Line numbers refer to main.tex as of 2026-10-26.*

Scope: 9 papers, read from the pdftotext copies in scratchpad/papers/. main.tex was read in full (250 lines), with focus on Sec. 1, 2, 3 (theory), 5 and 6.

### Theory cluster: cross-paper verdict on novelty

1. **"E_1 returns the (conditional) mean" is prior art, and stated in so many words.** Liu et al. (rectified flow), Fig. 5 caption: *"The rectified flow travels in straight lines and progresses uniformly in time; it generates the mean of π1 when simulated with a single Euler step, and quickly covers the whole distribution π1 with more steps (in this case N = 2 is sufficient)."* Their Table 1(a) gives the empirical counterpart. One-step 1-rectified flow on CIFAR-10 has **IS 1.13, FID 378, recall 0.0**. EDM Sec. 3 says the same for the σ(t)=t ODE: *"An immediate consequence is that at any x and t, a single Euler step to t = 0 yields the denoised image Dθ(x; t)."* Their Fig. 1 caption adds: *"With increasing noise level, the result approaches dataset mean."* main.tex Sec. 3 ("One Euler step returns the conditional mean") currently cites neither. **It must cite Liu Fig. 5/Table 1 and EDM Sec. 3, and present E_1 as a known fact, not a finding.**
2. **The Gaussian per-step Euler factor is prior art (Sabour et al. 2024, via Stancevic).** Stancevic App. C, proof of Thm C.1 (data N(0,c²I), EDM SDE, deterministic DDIM, which they note is "exactly the same update as in ODE Euler method (equation 18 in Sabour et al. (2024))"): x_{t_{i-1}} = (c² + t_{i-1} t_i)/(c² + t_i²) · x_{t_i}. I checked algebraically that this equals main.tex's per-step factor ⟨u_i,u_{i+1}⟩/‖u_i‖² after the change of variables σ=(1−t)/t, c=s_d (RF Euler = DDIM). Sabour Thm 3.1 (quoted in Stancevic eq. 6) also shows that the **KL-optimal Euler/DDIM grid is uniform in arctan(σ/c)**, i.e. equal angles θ_i in main.tex's notation. The cos-product form and the explicit "≤1 ⇒ contraction" reading do not appear in Stancevic. Whether they appear in Sabour I cannot say, because I did not read Sabour.
   - **New consequence (mine, checked numerically):** the angles sum to π/2 and log cos is concave on [0,π/2). Jensen's inequality therefore gives, **on any grid, ∏ cos θ_i ≤ cos(π/2k)^k**, with equality for the equal-angle (Sabour / rescaled-entropic) grid. Values: k=2: 0.500; k=4: 0.729; k=8: 0.856; k=10: 0.883. main.tex's uniform-t numbers sit below this bound: s=1 gives 0.879 at k=10 and 0.720 at k=4; s=0.5 gives 0.40/0.68/0.86 at k=2/4/10, which I reproduced exactly. **So no k-step Euler grid can keep more than cos(π/2k)^k of a Gaussian coordinate's deviation, and the KL-optimal schedule is the one that maximises the factor.** This is a clean bridge between "schedules" and "temperature". It also predicts that a schedule which improves few-step quality on Gaussian-like targets does so partly by *raising* the effective temperature, which fits App. schedules, where a schedule gain vanished at matched temperature.
3. **"Few-step / deterministic samplers have the wrong spread" is anticipated in pieces, but nobody calls it temperature or tests it at matched temperature.** Four pieces:
   - Analytic-DPM Thm 2: handcrafted reverse variances, including DDIM's λ²=0, *underestimate* the KL-optimal reverse variance. The gap's coefficient "can be large if we use a shorter trajectory". This is a statement about per-step kernels, not about output spread.
   - EDM Sec. 4 attributes detail loss to denoisers that "remove slightly too much noise — most likely due to regression toward the mean", and fixes it with S_noise ∈ {1.003, 1.007}.
   - Ning: the ε-norm is *over*-predicted during sampling. The fix scales ε down by b>1, the optimal b grows as the step count falls, and Euler needs a larger b than Heun.
   - Liu: one-step recall is 0.0.

   None of them measure output spread as a function of k, define an effective temperature, or compare a few-step sampler with a reference run at matched temperature. **Defensible novelty: the matched-temperature equivalence test and the driving-decision evidence. Not the contraction itself.**
4. **Temperature definitions differ.** Glow scales the latent std by T, which is exact for additive coupling only. BigGAN *truncates* (resamples |z| above a threshold) and does not scale. main.tex's τz is the Glow definition. The main.tex sentence that groups both as "scaling the prior noise" is imprecise for BigGAN.

- **Access:** full text (lipman.txt): method (Sec. 3–4, eqs. 5–23), experiments (Sec. 6, Table 1, Figs. 4–7), appendix hyper-parameters.

### Physics/planners cluster: cross-cutting answers
- **Is the Proposition anticipated?** Not by Biroli or Raya. Neither discusses integrators, equivariance of numerical
  maps, or the side of the symmetry hyperplane under discretization. Elsewhere the core fact is classical: Runge–Kutta
  and B-series methods are affine-equivariant, so they commute with g and preserve its fixed set. This is
  geometric-integration folklore (e.g. McLachlan et al., "B-series methods are exactly the affine equivariant
  methods", Numer. Math. 2016; I recall this from memory and have not read it in this pass). The Prop. should cite or
  acknowledge this and claim only the decision-level corollary. Caveats for the appendix proof: (a) E_1 has h·Lip=1 and
  sends everything to E[x1|c]∈H, the boundary case. (b) For well-separated symmetric modes, Lip v(·,t) near H grows like
  t m²/(1−t)³, so the condition fails well before t=1, not only "near t=1" as the TODO says. A sketch for two deltas ±m:
  E[x1|x]=m tanh(t m x/(1−t)²) and v=(E[x1|x]−x)/(1−t). (c) The homotopy-in-h argument needs Φ_h to stay injective for
  all h' ≤ h, which follows if the Lipschitz bound holds uniformly.
- **Speciation and few-step samplers:** Biroli say nothing about this. Raya show few-step DDIM/PNDM lose FID and
  diversity, and that starting late (skipping the mean-reverting phase) helps. That is consistent with "early steps
  matter for diversity". It also suggests that our E_k contraction is created mainly in the first steps, where the
  rectified-flow analogue of speciation sits (t_s≈1/(1+√Λ), my derivation).
- **Do driving planners report better scores with fewer steps?** DiffusionDrive: no, it is flat (87.9/88.1/88.1 for
  1/2/3 steps), and its 20→2 gain is a model change. GoalFlow: yes, mildly (90.3 at 5 steps vs 89.9 at 20, 88.9 at 1). It
  is the better motivating example for our protocol, but its metric is scorer-selected over 128–256 samples.
