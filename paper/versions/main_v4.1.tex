\begin{abstract}
Flow-matching driving policies are made faster by integrating with fewer Euler steps, and the change is usually reported as preserving or improving accuracy. We show that, at the level of the distributions they produce, few-step Euler samplers behave like an accurate reference solver run at a lower sampling temperature. One Euler step returns the conditional mean, and more steps contract samples toward it by an amount that a closed-form Gaussian factor matches at the median. On \NClips{} clips from the PhysicalAI-AV test split, the released 10-step samplers of three Alpamayo generations share an effective temperature of \AOneFiveETenTau--\ROneETenTau, and the reference solver at that temperature reproduces \ExplMin--\ExplMax\% of their change in single-sample error and in the rate of changed driving decisions, while second-order Heun samplers with a similar budget fail the same test. The match holds for rates over clips, not for individual samples, and any contraction toward the mean of the same size would pass it; temperature is the explicit knob that reproduces it. We therefore propose that accuracy gains from fewer steps be reported against a reference solver at matched effective temperature, and we give a protocol and its statistics for doing so.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Integrating Alpamayo-1.5 with four Euler steps instead of the released ten lowers its single-sample error against the human trajectory and changes about one decision in ten, compared with an accurate reference solver (\cref{fig:teaser,tab:matched}). Should the improvement be credited to the faster sampler? We argue that it should not: the same improvement, and nearly the same decision changes, are obtained by keeping the reference solver and shrinking the initial noise.

The reason is elementary. Under rectified-flow interpolation~\citep{lipman2023flow,liu2023rectified} the initial velocity is $v(z,0)=\E[x_1\mid c]-z$, so a single Euler step returns the conditional mean, and more steps move toward the exact flow. A $k$-step Euler sampler therefore sits between the mean and the policy's distribution, the same place a lower sampling temperature puts it. Accelerations that build on first-order integration~\citep{flashdrive2026,probeflow2026,adavla2026,coda2026} inherit this temperature shift, and averaged metrics such as minADE register it as an accuracy gain.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{A matched-temperature evaluation protocol} for accelerated flow samplers: estimate the sampler's effective temperature $\taueff$ from its seed spread, compare it with the reference solver run at $\taueff$, and report effect, residual and equivalence with clip-level intervals (\cref{sec:protocol}).
  \item \textbf{Evidence on three generations of a production driving VLA} on real test data. The released samplers share $\taueff\approx0.83$; temperature reproduces \ExplMin--\ExplMax\% of the ADE$_1$ and flip-rate changes of $E_4$ and $E_{10}$; Heun-3 and Heun-5 fail the same test (\cref{fig:curves,tab:matched}).
  \item \textbf{A mechanism with a closed form.} Contraction toward the conditional mean (\cref{fig:mechanism}), the Gaussian factor $\prod_i\cos\theta_i$ that matches it at the median, and a symmetry condition under which no integrator changes a decision (\cref{sec:theory}).
  \item \textbf{A boundary.} Qwen-Drive's planners are nearly deterministic; there the solver adds spread rather than removing it, and the temperature view does not apply.
\end{itemize}

\section{Background and Notation}
\label{sec:background}

\paragraph{Flow-matching driving policies.} A VLA driving policy encodes cameras, ego history and a reasoning trace into a context $c$ and decodes an action $x_1$ (64 normalized acceleration and curvature commands over 6.4\,s in Alpamayo) by integrating $v_\theta(x,t\mid c)$ from $x_0=z\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I)$ to $t=1$. With $x_t=(1-t)z+tx_1$, the flow-matching minimizer is $v(x,t)=\E[x_1-z\mid x_t=x,c]$. The released policies use forward Euler with 10 uniform steps~\citep{alpamayo2025,qwendrive2026}.

\paragraph{Notation.} $E_k$ is Euler with $k$ uniform steps; Heun-$k$ uses $2k$ field evaluations. $\Atau$ is the reference solver, Heun with 32 steps (64 evaluations), started from $\tau z$; $\Aref$ is the reference at $\tau=1$. All samplers for a clip share the same context, reasoning trace and six noises. $\Spr(S)$ is the mean pairwise distance between the trajectories $S$ produces from different noises. A \emph{flip} is a change of decision class relative to $\Aref$ under the same noise. ADE$_1$ is the mean single-sample displacement to the logged human trajectory.

\paragraph{Accelerations.} FlashDrive reuses the velocity on steps 3--6 of an 8-step Euler schedule~\citep{flashdrive2026}; ProbeFlow and AdaVLA adapt the number of steps~\citep{probeflow2026,adavla2026}; entropic time places steps by information about the output~\citep{stancevic2025entropic}; Coda corrects a few-step endpoint~\citep{coda2026}.

\section{Why Euler Steps Act Like Temperature}
\label{sec:theory}

\paragraph{One Euler step returns the conditional mean.} Since $x_0=z$, $E_1(z)=z+v(z,0)=\E[x_1\mid c]$ for every $z$: the one-step sampler has no diversity at all.

\paragraph{Gaussian targets: contraction on any grid.} For $p(x_1\mid c)=\mathcal{N}(m,\operatorname{diag}(s^2))$ the field is linear and coordinate $d$ evolves with scale $\|u_d(t)\|$, where $u_d(t)=(1-t,\,ts_d)$. One Euler step from $t_i$ to $t_{i+1}$ multiplies coordinate $d$'s deviation from $m_d$ by $\langle u_d(t_i),u_d(t_{i+1})\rangle/\|u_d(t_i)\|^2$, whereas the exact flow multiplies it by $\|u_d(t_{i+1})\|/\|u_d(t_i)\|$. Writing $\theta_{d,i}$ for the angle between $u_d(t_i)$ and $u_d(t_{i+1})$,
\begin{equation}
  \frac{E_k(z)_d-m_d}{\Aref(z)_d-m_d}=\prod_{i=0}^{k-1}\cos\theta_{d,i}\;\le\;1
  \label{eq:contraction}
\end{equation}
on any grid (\cref{fig:mechanism}a). The inequality is implicit in the proof of Proposition~2 of~\citet{claustre2026gaussian}; we use the closed form as a prediction. For $s_d\ge0.3$ the factor of $E_{10}$ lies between 0.82 and 0.88. Coordinates with smaller $s_d$ contract more but carry little of the spread, so the aggregate factor is set by the high-variance coordinates.

\paragraph{Decisions protected by symmetry do not flip.}
\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:symmetry}
Let $g$ be an orthogonal reflection with fixed hyperplane $H$, let $p(x_1\mid c)$ be $g$-invariant, and let the decision be the side of $H$. An explicit Runge--Kutta integrator whose steps satisfy $h\,\mathrm{Lip}(v(\cdot,t))<1$ returns the decision of the exact flow for almost every $z$.
\end{proposition}
\noindent The field is $g$-equivariant and hence tangent to $H$ on $H$; each step is an equivariant homeomorphism mapping $H$ to itself and depending continuously on $h$, so half-spaces are preserved. Decision flips therefore need an asymmetric context. \TODO{full proof in the appendix; the Lipschitz condition can fail near $t=1$, discuss.}

\section{Matched-Temperature Protocol and Setup}
\label{sec:protocol}
\begin{definition}
The effective temperature of a sampler $S$ is the $\tau$ at which $\Spr(\Atau)=\Spr(S)$, interpolated over $\tau\in\{1,0.9,\dots,0.4\}$.
\end{definition}
For a metric $M$ we report $M(\Aref)$, $M(S)$ and $M(A_{\taueff(S)})$. The \emph{residual} $M(S)-M(A_{\taueff(S)})$ is the part of the sampler's effect that temperature does not explain, and $1-\text{residual}/\text{effect}$ is the fraction explained. Clips are the independent unit: every quantity, including $\taueff$, is recomputed in each of 1000 bootstrap resamples of clips, drawn jointly across models because all models see the same clips. We call $S$ \emph{equivalent} to $\At{\taueff}$ on a metric when the 95\% interval of the residual lies inside a margin declared in advance (2 points for flip rates, 0.05\,m for ADE$_1$). Fractions explained above 100\% mean that the explicit temperature overshoots the sampler's effect; we therefore read residuals and their intervals, not fractions, as evidence. The protocol compares distributions: the same noise can still produce different decisions under $S$ and $\At{\taueff}$ (\cref{sec:discussion}).

\paragraph{Budgets.} The reference solver uses 64 field evaluations (NFE). $E_k$ uses $k$; Heun-3 uses 6 and Heun-5 uses 10, so Heun-5 has the released sampler's budget and Heun-3 sits between $E_4$ and $E_{10}$.

\paragraph{Policies.} Alpamayo-1.5, Alpamayo-R1 and Alpamayo-2-Super, frozen; Qwen-Drive-1.0-4B (SFT and RL planners) as a boundary case. Our integrators run inside each model's own sampling call with the model's step function, so the reasoning trace is shared across samplers within a clip.
\paragraph{Data.} The same \NClips{} clips from the PhysicalAI-AV test split~\citep{physicalai2026} for every model (one clip per dataset chunk, sampled before any analysis), six noise seeds each, with the current frame at 5.1\,s.
\paragraph{Decisions.} Longitudinal (stop, decelerate, keep, accelerate) and lateral (left, right, lane shift, straight) classes are read from each plan with thresholds fixed in advance (\cref{app:protocols}).

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}
\paragraph{Accelerating VLA sampling.} FlashDrive, ProbeFlow, AdaVLA and Coda reduce or correct flow sampling and report trajectory or success metrics~\citep{flashdrive2026,probeflow2026,adavla2026,coda2026}. Our protocol separates the part of such gains that a temperature change reproduces.
\paragraph{Discretization and diversity.} Few-step generators lose rare modes in image synthesis~\citep{subflow2026}, few-step error has been tied to single-Gaussian assumptions~\citep{chen2025gmflow}, and Euler on Gaussian flows has been analyzed by \citet{claustre2026gaussian}. We study the consequence for driving decisions.
\paragraph{Critical windows and schedules.} Speciation and symmetry breaking in diffusion~\citep{biroli2024dynamical,raya2023ssb} and entropic time~\citep{stancevic2025entropic} motivate where steps should go. A schedule that targets decision information passed a pre-registered confirmation on 436 clips not used to design it, yet its gain disappeared at matched temperature (\cref{app:schedules}); this negative result motivated the protocol.

\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}
\paragraph{Recommendations.} Report $\taueff$ for any first-order or cached sampler, and compare against the reference solver at that temperature before attributing accuracy gains to the sampler. If lower temperature is desired, set it explicitly so that it is visible and tunable.
\paragraph{Distribution, not trajectory.} Matching holds for rates averaged over clips. Under the same noise, $E_4$ and the reference solver at $\taueff$ still disagree on a sizable share of individual decisions, and per-clip rates differ, so the two are not interchangeable sample by sample or clip by clip.
\paragraph{What the test identifies.} Matching spread and then comparing rates does not single out temperature. A post-hoc shrinkage of reference samples toward $E_1$ with the same spread also passes it. We tested this directly. Moving each reference sample linearly toward $E_1$ until its spread matches $E_k$'s (Shrink$_k$ in \cref{tab:matched}) gives flip residuals of \ShrinkFlipResidMin{} to \ShrinkFlipResidMax{} points, all with intervals that contain zero, just as Euler does. The two differ per sample: shrinkage contracts every sample by the same factor (median \ShrinkContrFour{} at $E_4$'s spread, \ShrinkContrTen{} at $E_{10}$'s), whereas Euler contracts 90--93\% of samples and less on the median (\cref{find:contraction}). What the evidence supports is that few-step Euler is a contraction toward the conditional mean, of the size the Gaussian factor predicts, that rate-level evaluation cannot tell apart from other contractions of the same size, and that explicit temperature is a visible and tunable way to obtain the same rates. Heun fails because it changes decisions without contracting.
\paragraph{Limitations.} Decision classes are read from plans with fixed thresholds rather than from executed behavior; the reasoning trace is fixed within a clip; \NClips{} clips do not establish equivalence at tight margins; rare decisions are untested; the positive replication comes from one model family, with Qwen-Drive as a different-family boundary case. \TODO{multi-seed language ablation; ReCogDrive and DiffusionDrive on NAVSIM.}

\section*{Impact Statement}
This work concerns how accelerated autonomous-driving policies are evaluated. Gains that come from a hidden temperature change can hide shifts in the decision distribution, and reporting effective temperature makes such shifts visible. We used public data and frozen public checkpoints; no vehicles were driven.
